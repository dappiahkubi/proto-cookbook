The spatial and temporal analysis of total precipitation at tropical South America indicates a distinct pattern over two time periods: 1986 to 2005 and 2080 to 2100 under the high emission scenario SSP585. The mean total precipitation maps reveal that areas of peak precipitation remain generally consistent, largely concentrated near the equator and along the western coast. However, the future period under SSP585 shows a tendency for slightly increased precipitation intensity in some regions compared to the historical period.
The time series plots of monthly and mean precipitation further illustrate variability with some increase in mean precipitation values towards the end of the 21st century. This suggests a potential intensification of the hydrological cycle in tropical South America under high greenhouse gas emissions, with monthly precipitation flux showing significant natural variability but a slight upward trend in mean precipitation.
This analysis highlights the importance of continued monitoring and modeling to understand regional climate change impacts on precipitation patterns, which are critical for water resources and ecosystem management in tropical South America.
